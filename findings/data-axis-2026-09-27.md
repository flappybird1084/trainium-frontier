# The data axis, priced and closed (2026-09-27)

**VERDICT: this axis is worth less than 0.001 and most of it is worth less than one gate's noise floor.**
Every data-side mechanism that changes *which* tokens we train on or *how many times* we see them is capped at
**−0.0007 bpb** by the data-constrained scaling law, and the specific "get to 100% corpus coverage" family is
capped at **−0.00023** — which is *below* the ±0.00025 per-gate noise floor, so it is not merely small, it is
**unmeasurable**. Eight full 1800 s gates already tested this family and all eight lost or washed. The only
sub-axes the arithmetic does not cap are **ordering/curriculum** and **self-scored reweighting**, and both are
(a) measured to have no existing defect to fix — the corpus is already an i.i.d. shuffle — and (b) capped at
about −0.001 by the most optimistic published fixed-compute curriculum results.

**Do not spend the next gate here.** If a gate is spent here anyway, §6 gives the single best design.

This file is research + local analysis only. Nothing was run on the box. Numbers marked **MEASURED** were
computed locally against the cached train shards this session; numbers marked **LEDGER** are existing full-length
gates; numbers marked **DERIVED** are arithmetic.

## 1. The bound that closes most of the axis

Muennighoff et al., [*Scaling Data-Constrained Language Models*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264), fit an
effective-data law: with `U_D` unique tokens processed `1+R_D` times,

```
D_effective = U_D · (1 + R_D* · (1 − exp(−R_D / R_D*)))      R_D* = 15.387 (their fit)
```

**Our exact position** (MEASURED corpus size, LEDGER step count):

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| corpus (5 train shards, provided tokenizer) | **315,947,189 tokens / 424,960 documents** |
| tokens trained (4390 steps × 131,072) | **575.4M** |
| nominal epochs | **1.821** |
| unique tokens actually trained on (87.0% coverage) | **274.9M** |
| epochs over the tokens we *do* use | **2.093** |
| marginal value of a repeated token here | **94.8% of a fresh token** |

Converting effective-token e-folds to bpb at our measured **−0.0353 bpb per e-fold** (the end-to-end
pure-throughput v46→v55 series; `quality-levers-2026-09-26.md` §5):

| hypothetical | Δ bpb (DERIVED) |
|---|---|
| fix the packer's truncation waste (87.0% → 91.0% coverage) | **−0.00008** |
| perfect 100% corpus coverage — the BFD/stream ideal | **−0.00023** |
| remove every near-duplicate we can find (0.03% of tokens) | **−0.00000** |
| **magic: all 575.4M trained tokens unique (needs 575M of fresh corpus, impossible)** | **−0.00065** |

**Which bound this is: the generous one.** The −0.0353/e-fold slope was measured on *step-count* changes, which
buy more optimizer updates as well as more tokens. A pure data-uniqueness change buys only the data term, so the
true numbers are *smaller* than the table. I am deliberately giving the optimistic side and it still closes.

**Sensitivity, because `R_D*` was fitted on C4 at larger unique-data pools than ours** (DERIVED):

| `R_D*` | max from 100% coverage | max from all-unique |
|---|---|---|
| **15.39 (published fit)** | **−0.00023** | −0.00065 |
| 8.0 | −0.00043 | −0.00123 |
| 4.0 | −0.00082 | −0.00238 |
| 2.0 | −0.00152 | −0.00451 |
| 1.0 | −0.00262 | −0.00808 |

You need `R_D*` to be **8x more pessimistic than published** before coverage work reaches −0.0015. And our own
ledger argues the opposite direction: WD 0.2/0.3 (+0.0045/+0.0109) and MIR 0.05/0.10/0.15
(+0.0050/+0.0096/+0.0139) were *monotonically harmful*, and those regularizers only pay once repetition actually
bites. Two independent lines therefore say we are in the large-`R_D*`, undertrained-not-overfit corner.

**Consequence for the framing in the brief:** "we are in the repeat regime, and that is unexplored" is true but
the regime is nearly free. The total penalty for being at 1.82 epochs instead of 1.82 epochs of fresh data is
**0.00065 bpb**. Any mechanism whose story is "make the repeat pass behave more like fresh data" inherits that
cap. That includes cached-logit self-distillation, epoch-2 repacking, epoch-2 reordering, dedup, and every
coverage fix.

`P_ROLL_EP2` came back +0.0004.

## 3. New MEASURED facts about the corpus (local, this session)

### 3.1 Document lengths, and the cliff that defines our coverage

424,960 documents, mean **743.5** tokens, median **618**.

| percentile | 1 | 5 | 25 | 50 | 75 | 90 | 95 | 99 | 99.9 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tokens | 51 | 100 | 284 | **618** | 823 | 1113 | 1743 | 4796 | 16132 | 155351 |

| threshold | documents | share of docs | **share of tokens** |
|---|---|---|---|
| > 1025 (PACK=1 row) | 48,212 | 11.35% | **38.50%** |
| > 2048 | 16,485 | 3.88% | 24.49% |
| > 4097 (PACK=4 row) | 5,526 | **1.30%** | **14.83%** |
| > 8192 | 1,644 | 0.39% | 8.01% |

The distribution is extremely heavy-tailed: 1.3% of documents hold 14.8% of the tokens.

### 3.2 The provided packer, simulated exactly (rank 0, 4390 steps)

I re-implemented `prepare.make_dataloader`'s inner loop and ran rank 0's real document order
(row groups `0, 8, 16, …` of the 415 total) for the full 17,560 rows of 4097:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| tokens emitted into training rows | 71,943,320 |
| documents placed whole | 95,546 |
| documents truncated | 15,793 (**0.90 per row**) |
| **tokens read then discarded by truncation** | **3,291,391 = 4.0% of tokens read** |
| **tokens stuck in the buffer, never trained on** | **7,439,815 = 9.0% of tokens read** |
| coverage | **87.0%** (reproduces the recorded 87%) |
| documents per 4097-row | mean 6.34, median 6 |
| passes over rank 0's supply | **2.09** |

### 3.3 The buffer is 96% clogged — and it does not matter

`prepare.py:494` refills only while `len(doc_buffer) < 512`, and a document longer than the row capacity can
never satisfy `n <= remaining`, so it is never popped. Measured steady state: **533 documents in the buffer, of
which 513 are un-placeable** (all > 4097, median length 10,648). The buffer never grows without bound — it
saturates — but it means **best-fit is choosing from roughly 20–128 candidate documents, not 512**. The loader
docstring says "best-fit packed"; in practice it is best-fit over a pool that is 96% dead weight.

This is a real defect and it is already measured to be worth nothing: `P_MAXDOC4097` removes exactly this
pollution and returned +0.0005.

### 3.4 Deduplication: measured dead

Blake2b over each document's token sequence, plus 32-permutation MinHash over 8-gram shingles, 8 bands of 4:

- **exact duplicate documents: 49 of 424,960 = 0.012%**, holding **0.003% of tokens** (37 groups, largest 5).
- **near-duplicates at estimated Jaccard ≥ 0.8: 208 documents = 0.049%**, holding **0.031% of tokens**.
- Caveat: 8×4 banding has ~40% recall at Jaccard 0.5, so the true moderate-near-dup mass could be several times
  higher. Even at 10x it is 0.3% of tokens, which §1 prices at **−0.00001 bpb**.

ClimbMix is already deduplicated upstream. **There is no dedup lever. Do not build one.**

### 3.5 The corpus is already an i.i.d. shuffle — measured two ways

This kills the whole "the existing presentation order is accidentally bad" hypothesis.

- **Length autocorrelation** of `log(doc length)` at document lags 1, 2, 5, 10, 50, 1024, 10240:
  **−0.0024, +0.0006, −0.0029, +0.0000, −0.0020, +0.0010, +0.0028.** Zero at every lag.
- **Row-group mean log-length** varies with sd **0.0268** across the 415 row groups against **0.0279** predicted
  by i.i.d. sampling. Indistinguishable from random assignment.
- **Content**: per-row-group unigram distributions over the 8192-token vocabulary, Jensen–Shannon divergence:
  **adjacent row groups 0.01291, random row-group pairs 0.01287, same-shard 0.01287, cross-shard 0.01285.**
  Adjacent row groups are *not* more similar than random ones.

So there is **no domain/topic/source block structure** in the provided order. Reshuffling is a no-op in
distribution. A curriculum here is not a repair, it is a construction from scratch, with no free lunch from
fixing a pathology — because there is no pathology.

### 3.6 Document masking makes in-row composition a no-op

`doc_bounds()` gives each position the `[start, end)` of its own document and the attention kernels intersect that
with the causal limit, so **a document in a packed row cannot attend to any other document in that row at all.**
Consequences, both of which answer questions in the brief by construction rather than by gate:

- **Relatedness-aware packing (`compile-window-and-packing.md` B4, never run) is dead by construction.** The
  literature result it rests on ([2402.13991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13991),
  [In-Context Pretraining](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10638)) requires cross-document attention *inside* the
  packed sequence; that same paper finds intra-document masking gives the lowest perplexity, and our own ledger
  measured masking at **−0.009 and 15% faster**. Relatedness and masking are alternatives, not a stack, and we
  are on the better side of the trade.
- **"Let epoch 2 see a different packing of the same documents" is a no-op** except for where the 1024-token
  sub-row boundaries fall relative to documents — which is precisely what `P_ROLL` (+0.0007) and `P_ROLL_EP2`
  (+0.0004) measured.
- Only leak: the hashed bigram table reads `prev = [BOS, idx[:, :-1]]`, so the BOS position of each document gets
  a cross-document bigram. That is 6.34 BOS per 4097 tokens = **0.15% of positions**, and eval does the same.

### 3.7 Packing vs padding, priced without a gate

One document per 1024-row with padding wastes `1 − 743/1024 = 27%` of positions, i.e. 27% fewer trained tokens at
identical device work = 0.315 e-folds = **+0.011 bpb**. The nearest measured point is PACK=1 at +0.0045. Packing
multiple documents per row is not a close call.

### Priced out without a gate (record so they are not rediscovered)

- **Cached-logit self-distillation on the repeat pass** — the last survivor of `repeat-regime.md`. Already parked
  on cost in `ideas.md` item 4: the fused NKI head never materialises logits, so both the pass-1 cache and the
  pass-2 soft loss need the logits path, one graph, always on = **+10–12% step = +0.003 to +0.0035 to recover**.
  §1 now adds the second kill: its entire prize is the repeat-pass penalty, which is **−0.00065**. Dead twice.
- **Using train shards 5…6541** — not obtainable: `PUBLIC_TRAIN_SHARDS = list(range(5))` is pinned in the fixed
  `prepare.py`, `check_submission` bans every networking import, and the scoring host caches only shards 0–4.
  It would also be an external-data violation. Do not pursue.
- **Byte-weighted or exact-bpb loss reweighting** — `evaluate_bpb` is `nats / (ln2 · bytes)`, i.e. exactly
  uniform per-token cross-entropy, which is what we already minimise. Closed twice before; confirmed again.
- **Rank data imbalance** — 415 row groups over 8 ranks means ranks 0–6 get 52 and rank 7 gets 51 (−1.9% of
  supply). At 2.09 epochs this shifts rank 7's epoch boundary, nothing else. Worth ~0.

## 5. The one number to keep

**The analytic ceiling on coverage/packing/dedup/repetition work (−0.00023) is smaller than one gate's noise
floor (sd ≈ 0.0002–0.00025, `repeat-regime.md` §3).** Even a perfect, free implementation could not be
*distinguished from zero* without roughly n=8 paired gates. That is the cleanest reason to stop: not that the
effect is negative, but that it is unobservable with our instrument.

## 6. If a gate is spent here anyway: the single best design

The only question with unbounded upside is **does presentation order matter at all at this scale**, and the
cheapest way to settle it is one maximal, confound-free perturbation.

**`D_ORDER`: same rows, different time order.** In the excluded startup window (≈400 s spare against the 900 s
cap; tokenising one rank's 39.4M tokens took 13 s locally and the packing simulation seconds), pre-materialise
the full per-rank row plan with the *unchanged* packer, score each 4097-row by mean token surprisal under a
unigram+bigram counting model built from the same pass, then present the rows **sorted ascending by surprisal**
(easy → hard) instead of in packing order. Identical tokens, identical packing, identical coverage, identical
row contents — only the time index changes. Zero device cost.

- **Why this arm:** it is the maximal ordering intervention that holds every other variable fixed. Sorting by
  document length instead would confound with packing quality and coverage.
- **Falsifier:** |Δ| < 0.0005 at full length ⇒ the model is order-insensitive at 4390 steps and the entire
  ordering/curriculum axis closes. A large move in *either* direction (easy→hard helping or hurting) makes it
  live and worth a second arm (descending).
- **Implementation risk:** the row plan must stay per-rank disjoint (the `F_V24_EP2` failure mode) and shapes must
  stay fixed so no NEFF recompiles mid-run. Roughly a day. `submission_raw/train3_pack.py` already owns a
  custom packer to build on.
- **Honest expectation:** 0. I would not spend the gate. With three days left and the ranked non-data queue
  holding per-group terminal averaging (−0.002 … −0.004), EMA-Nesterov lookahead (≈ −0.0018 published, built and
  gating) and five never-swept architecture constants, every one of those has a larger expected value than
  anything on this axis — and **v72/v73 is still not uploaded, which is ~−0.0023 of portal margin for free.**
